## ENGLISH TITLE, ABSTRACT AND KEYWORDS

**Incretin-based therapies in paediatric and adolescent gynaecology: the effect of GLP-1 receptor agonists on the adolescent reproductive axis**

Paediatric and adolescent obesity is no longer a comorbidity in the gynaecological consultation room but a starting point: premature adrenarche, early menarche, oligomenorrhoea, the hyperandrogenic phenotype and later reproductive failure share a common metabolic root. The licensing of glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists above the age of 12 years has, for the first time, provided a pharmacological means of breaking the body-mass trajectory and thereby indirectly remodelling the reproductive axis. This review summarises the available evidence for girls aged 8 to 18 years across five effect domains: body weight and body composition, steroid hormone profile, sexual maturation and menstrual function, inflammation, and lifetime risk. Randomised evidence for weight is robust: in adolescents aged 12 to 18 years, once-weekly semaglutide reduced body-mass index by 16.1% over 68 weeks compared with a 0.6% increase on placebo, and almost three-quarters of participants achieved at least 5% weight loss; liraglutide was effective in both the 12–18 and the 6–12 year age bands. Data on steroid hormones and menstrual function derive predominantly from adult populations with polycystic ovary syndrome, in whom GLP-1 receptor agonists improve menstrual frequency, lower androgen concentrations and raise sex hormone-binding globulin; direct adolescent evidence is sparse. The anti-inflammatory effect is measurable and partly independent of weight loss. Essentially no human data address the timing of sexual maturation, whereas animal experiments indicate direct modulation of the gonadal axis, which warrants caution during puberty. Practical considerations centre on contraception and a pre-conception washout, screening for eating disorders, protection of bone and lean mass, and weight regain after discontinuation. The review closes with a life-course model following three trajectories – a girl of normal body habitus, an untreated girl with obesity, and a girl with obesity treated with a GLP-1 receptor agonist and maintained on therapy – from prepuberty to old age, illustrating the avertable disease burden and its health-economic weight.

**Keywords:** GLP-1 receptor agonist, adolescent obesity, polycystic ovary syndrome, reproductive axis, health economics
